# Selection, not generation, decides synthetic HAM10000 gains

Diffusion-based augmentation of HAM10000/ISIC gives real but modest gains. Verified HAM10000 results are around **+3 points of accuracy** in a reduced-data regime, and dermatology-wide evidence shows gains that **saturate above about 10:1 synthetic:real** and stay well below the value of adding real images. The open questions sit downstream of the generator: which synthetic images to keep, and how many. On selection, almost every published filter uses one or two signals: classifier agreement with the intended label and/or feature-space proximity to real data. These filters use fixed thresholds, and the judge is often the downstream classifier or a sibling of it. That design risks confirmation bias, because it favours canonical, easy samples that add little information. On quantity, the best-documented augmentation curve rises, peaks near a small multiple of the real set, and then declines. Per-class optima are well motivated, but no HAM10000 study sweeps per-class counts across multiple seeds with family-wise error control. On imbalance, nv makes up about two thirds of HAM10000, and image-level splits leak lesion siblings. Many published accuracy figures are therefore inflated, and balanced accuracy on a lesion-grouped split is the honest metric. On judge bias, theory and adjacent evidence show that scoring models carry their training prior and label space into their judgements. No paper documents the specific failure this project observed, a majority-class "nv sink" when a dermoscopy classifier scores synthetic minority-class images. ASISM therefore addresses three genuine gaps: multi-signal sample selection with a judge independent of the downstream classifier, a pre-registered multi-seed quantity curve (E4) that decides counts, and an explicit audit of judge bias. Its IQA, MC-dropout and Grad-CAM components have no direct precedent in the retrieved notes, so the thesis can neither borrow support for them nor be accused of duplicating prior work there.

## Diffusion augmentation on HAM10000 buys about three points

The best-verified HAM10000 result comes from Kim et al. They modified SD v1-4 with an 8-channel VAE, CLIP adapters, a learnable class-label embedding and latent interpolation, then generated 500 images per class. Across nine classifiers, mean accuracy rose from **80.92% with 500 real images to 84.15% with 500 real plus 500 synthetic (+3.23 pp)**. Synthetic-only training reached **76.63%**, and zero-shot transfer to PAD-UFES-20 collapsed to **49.89-64.27%**. Per-class results and melanoma recall were not reported ([Kim et al. 2025, PLoS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12494260/)). A 2024 IEEE conference paper reports +4-5% macro recall from adding synthetic images at 1-1.5x the real-set size. That attribution comes from a merged search summary and could not be verified against the full text ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10499667/)). LoRA and DreamBooth fine-tuning of Stable Diffusion on HAM10000 or ISIC is common, for example in a World Scientific journal paper ([IJITDM](https://www.worldscientific.com/doi/10.1142/S0219622025500567)) and in Derm-T2IM, which trains on 2,800 binary-labelled images ([arXiv 2401.05159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05159)). However, **no verifiable SDXL+LoRA paper on HAM10000 with downstream classifier results was found**. The thesis's generator choice is therefore under-documented in the literature rather than contradicted by it.

The strongest dermatology-wide quantity evidence is Sagers et al. They generated **458,920 synthetic images** and found that gains plateau above a **10:1 synthetic:real ratio** and are "substantially smaller than the gains obtained from adding real images" ([Sagers et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12453)). The high-profile positive results are mostly about fairness across skin tone and demographic subgroups, not diagnosis classes. Ktena et al. found that label-conditioned diffusion improved robustness and fairness in dermatology, histopathology and chest X-ray, with the largest gains for underrepresented groups. Dermatologists diagnosed their synthetic dermatology images about as accurately as real ones ([Ktena et al. 2024, Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02838-6)). Similar subgroup-focused findings come from DALL-E 2 augmentation across skin types ([Sagers et al. 2022](https://mlanthology.org/neuripsw/2022/sagers2022neuripsw-improving/)), LesionGen's concept-guided synthesis ([arXiv 2507.23001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.23001)) and cgDDI. cgDDI expanded DDI more than 400-fold and reports **+13.9% cross-dataset accuracy** on Fitzpatrick17k ([arXiv 2607.12987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12987)).

Per-diagnosis-class evidence is thinner. SkinGenBench (StyleGAN2-ADA vs DDPM on 14,116 HAM10000+MILK10K images) reports **melanoma F1 gains of 8-15 points**. It also finds that DDPM samples have higher variance but lower fidelity than GAN samples, and that heavy artifact removal brings only marginal benefit ([SkinGenBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17585)). Controllable diffusion synthesis of the six HAM10000 minority classes gives **+2.32 F1** on average ([Medical Image Analysis 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841526002604)). Evaluation practice is weak across the board. FID values range from about 65 to 145 and cannot be compared across papers. Expert reader studies are rare. Accuracy, which nv dominates, is the most common headline metric. No retrieved paper reports a net *loss* of balanced accuracy on HAM10000 from diffusion augmentation. That absence plausibly reflects publication bias rather than a property of the method.

The failure modes are well documented outside dermatology and only partly documented within it. Generators reproduce rulers, hair and ink ([Carrasco Limeros et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11702)). Unconditional latent diffusion models memorize patient data: in one study **37.2% of patient data was memorized and 68.7% of synthetic samples were copies** ([Nature Biomedical Engineering 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-025-01468-8)). Diffusion models memorize more than GANs ([arXiv 2305.07644](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.07644)). LoRA fine-tuning on HAM10000's df (115 images) and vasc (142 images) is exactly the small-data regime where memorization risk peaks, yet **no dermatology-specific memorization audit of SD/LoRA models was found**.

## Published filters use one or two signals and the classifier as judge

The prevailing selection signals fall into two families. The first is classifier agreement with the intended label. Akrout et al. generated **30,000 images per class**, removed non-skin images with a binary EfficientNet, and kept only images that an EfficientNetV2 + RegNet + Swin ensemble, pretrained on the real data, classified as the intended label ([Akrout et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04802)). He et al. filter with CLIP ([arXiv 2210.07574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07574)). The second family is feature-space proximity to real data. The closest medical precedent to a multi-signal selector is HistoGAN, which first keeps samples with low label entropy, then keeps the half nearest to the real class centroid in ResNet18 feature space. It reports **+6.7% (cervical) and +2.8% (metastasis) accuracy** ([Xue et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06399)). The one selection method evaluated directly on HAM10000 is CosSIF. It filters by cosine similarity both before GAN training and after generation, and reports **recall +13.75% on HAM10000** and +1.59% sensitivity on ISIC-2016 ([CosSIF, arXiv 2307.13842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13842)). Any thesis that proposes a HAM10000 selector has to discuss or benchmark CosSIF.

Newer general-vision work moves past per-sample label consistency. Post-generation HO/HE curation rewards semantic alignment and penalizes canonical redundancy, and matches real-data performance with **up to 40% fewer synthetic samples** ([arXiv 2607.02637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02637)). FROST scores each sample by how well its gradient aligns with real-data gradients. It filters out 20-30% of synthetic data for **+0.58 to 1.14%** on CIFAR-100 ([arXiv 2609.29988](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29988v1)). TADA synthesizes only for the 30-40% of real examples the model has not learned early in training ([arXiv 2505.21574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21574)). A high-dimensional analysis finds that **covariance shift, not mean shift**, between synthetic and target data drives generalization error, and that covariance matching beats competing selectors ([Rezaei et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08123)). Most landmark ImageNet-scale papers (Azizi, Sariyildiz, Real-Fake, DataDream, Diversify-Don't-Fine-Tune) improve the generator or the prompts rather than filtering samples ([Yu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02253); [Azizi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08466)). Sample-level fidelity and diversity metrics exist, including alpha-precision/beta-recall ([Alaa et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08921)), density/coverage ([Naeem et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09797)) and Vendi ([Friedman & Dieng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02410)). None was found in use as the per-sample filter for classifier augmentation.

The literature also shows when filtering backfires. ALIA *drops* edits that a classifier trained on the real data predicts confidently, treating them as uninformative ([ALIA](https://lisadunlap.github.io/alia-website/)). That is the opposite of a "keep confident samples" rule. FROST finds that aggressive, narrow utility bands throw away useful supervision. Generators over-produce canonical class modes ([HO/HE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02637)). A brain-MRI pipeline adds farthest-point sampling specifically to stop the filtered set collapsing onto near-identical high-density samples ([arXiv 2605.23094](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23094)). The covariance result implies that centroid-proximity filters, HistoGAN's included, can shrink spread when spread is what matters. Keep-rates that have worked cluster at moderate values: about 50% for HistoGAN, 70-80% for FROST and 20-60% for the OOD filter of Jiang et al. ([arXiv 2605.03221](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03221)). This supports ASISM's decision to combine fidelity with diversity and novelty rather than maximize agreement. It also warns that an ASISM composite weighted heavily on classifier confidence would reproduce the collapse the literature describes.

None of the retrieved notes contains literature on **no-reference IQA, MC-dropout uncertainty or Grad-CAM attribution as selection signals for synthetic medical images**. The nearest evidence is a chest X-ray study in which diffusion-classifier uncertainty, used to filter predictions, improved the accuracy of the remaining predictions ([arXiv 2502.03687](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03687)). That is uncertainty used at inference time, not for selecting training data. These three ASISM components are therefore novel by absence in this corpus. The thesis should still run its own targeted search before claiming priority, and it must justify each one empirically, since no external validation exists to lean on.

## Quantity curves peak, then fall, and the peak should differ by class

When synthetic images are added to a fixed real set, the canonical curve is non-monotone. In Azizi et al., ResNet-50 on ImageNet went from **76.39% with real data only to 78.12% with +1.2M synthetic (1x)**, then fell to 77.48% at 2x and 76.75% at 3x. The authors attribute the decline to generator bias ([Azizi et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08466)). These per-row figures came from a page summary and should be checked against Table 4. Synthetic-only scaling is a different regime. Fan et al. fit power laws that saturate at about **4M images for ViT-B**. They also split classes into "easy", "scaling" and "poor", where poor classes barely improve because the generator cannot render the concept ([Fan et al. CVPR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04567)). Sariyildiz et al. see gains up to 50x in synthetic-only training ([CVPR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08420)). Those numbers should not be used to predict augmentation curves. For closed-set generators trained on the same data, which is the HAM10000 LoRA case, the synthetic-to-real equivalence ratio changes with real-set size and with how much synthetic data is already present ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09550)). For HAM10000 this means a synthetic nv image (about 6.7k real) and a synthetic df image (115 real) have very different marginal value.

The medical-imaging optimum ranges widely: saturation above 10:1 in data-limited dermatology ([Sagers et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12453)), and no saturation even at 100% synthetic proportion in CheXpert, where **AUC rose from 0.911 to 0.932** and racial AUC gaps shrank from 0.062 to 0.010 ([Rehman et al. 2026](https://journals.plos.org/digitalhealth/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pdig.0001277)). On ISIC, the common default is fill-to-head. Jiang et al. fill every ISIC2019 class to NV's 12,875 images, so DF (239 real) receives about 12,636 synthetic images. Balanced accuracy rises from **0.757 to 0.802**, and DF recall from 0.611 to 0.880, but the results come from 5-fold CV with no confidence intervals ([arXiv 2605.03221](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03221)). Fill-to-head confounds "more data" with "more data where real data was thinnest", so it cannot show where the optimum lies. Adaptive allocation challenges full balancing. IOIS, evaluated on HAM10000, allocates more synthetic samples to classes with low training accuracy ([Li et al. MICCAI 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08407)). FedEAS recovers most of the gain of full balancing with **94.1% less generation budget** ([arXiv 2607.06616](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06616)). Both are the closest priors to ASISM's "how many" decision.

Methodology is the clearest gap. Most quantity curves are point estimates from single runs or k-fold means. The better medical papers use bootstrap CIs and paired tests, as Rehman et al. do with 250 resamples. Benchmark variance is large enough to flip conclusions ([Bouthillier et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03098)), and **no synthetic-augmentation paper was found that applies Holm ([Holm 1979](https://www.jstor.org/stable/4615733)) or any family-wise correction across quantity levels**. The self-consuming-loop literature is relevant only by analogy. E4 keeps all real data and adds synthetic on top, which is the "accumulate" regime where test error stays bounded ([Gerstgrasser et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413)). Even so, tails are lost first ([Shumailov et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y)), which predicts that a rare-class generator with collapsed variation produces a peak-then-decline curve.

## HAM10000's nv prior shapes both the metric and the judges

HAM10000 contains **6,705 nv images out of 10,015 (about 67%)**, against 115 df, giving an nv:df ratio of about 58:1 ([Tschandl et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2018.161); counts should be verified locally against the metadata). A constant-nv classifier scores about 67% accuracy but only 0.143 balanced accuracy, so accuracy says little. The ISIC 2018 Task 3 challenge ranked entries by balanced multiclass accuracy for this reason ([Codella et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03368)). The dataset contains only **about 7,470 unique lesions**. A non-peer-reviewed tutorial found that grouping splits by lesion lowers accuracy from 0.94 to 0.88 ([Galaxy tutorial](https://training.galaxyproject.org/training-material/topics/statistics/tutorials/image_learner/tutorial.html)). A non-peer-reviewed leakage audit reports that a random image-level split leaves siblings of about 40% of test images (about 70% of melanomas) in training, and that leakage-free models reach **about 0.69 BA** ([ham-triage](https://github.com/jackdoesjava/ham-triage)). Cassidy et al. document duplicates within and between ISIC releases, including train/test duplication ([Medical Image Analysis 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841521003509)). Even the ISIC 2018 winner, with ensembles and external data, reached **0.885 BMA but only 0.760 melanoma sensitivity** ([Mahbod et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169260719311460)). Mel/nv confusion therefore remains the dominant residual error at the top of the field. The project's lesion-grouped single-DenseNet BA of 0.58-0.60 (project records, not literature) sits at the low end. Comparing it with leaky published figures above 0.85 is not like-for-like.

Every imbalance remedy trades nv recall for minority recall, and the long-tail literature explains why. Cross-entropy argmax is Bayes-optimal for the training prior, not for balanced error. Post-hoc logit adjustment, which subtracts τ·log(prior), is consistent for BA ([Menon et al. ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07314)). Much long-tail bias sits in the classifier head's weight norms rather than in the features, and re-training only the head (cRT, τ-normalisation) corrects it ([Kang et al. ICLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)). Oversampling is generally the strongest simple remedy for CNNs ([Buda et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05381)). Deferred re-weighting beats re-weighting from the start ([Cao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07413)). For this thesis, the implication is that the project's V3a pattern (mel recall up, nv down, BA flat) looks like movement *along* the minority/majority trade-off curve, not a gain in discriminative information.

## Judges inherit their training prior, and no paper names the nv sink

Feature-based metrics encode their backbone's label space. Inception pre-logit features are one affine map away from ImageNet logits, so matching ImageNet class histograms lowers FID without improving quality ([Kynkäänniemi et al. ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06026)). Classification Accuracy Score (train on synthetic, test on real) exposed a **27.9% Top-1 drop** for BigGAN-deep that FID and IS missed, and it reveals per-class failures ([Ravuri & Vinyals 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10887)). In long-tailed self-training, confidence thresholds produce "an ever increasing bias towards the majority class" ([arXiv 2401.04435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04435); [He et al. ICCV 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/papers/He_Re-Distributing_Biased_Pseudo_Labels_for_Semi-Supervised_Semantic_Segmentation_A_Baseline_ICCV_2021_paper.pdf)). Filtering synthetic images with the classifier that will later be trained is the augmentation analogue of pseudo-label confirmation bias ([Arazo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02983)). Together with Menon's result, this gives a mechanism for the project's own observation, that synthetic mel/bkl images are read as nv: a judge trained on a 67%-nv prior sends ambiguous or slightly off-manifold inputs to nv even when the images are not bad. **No peer-reviewed paper documents this sink for dermoscopy**, so the observation is original to the thesis.

Switching to a foundation-model judge does not remove the problem. It relocates it. On natural images, DINOv2-ViT-L/14 tracks human realism judgements far better than Inception ([Stein et al. NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04675)). That finding concerns distribution-level metrics, not per-sample class assignment through a linear probe on seven imbalanced classes. In medical imaging, a MICCAI 2024 study of 16 StyleGAN2 models and 11 extractors found that ImageNet-trained extractors (SwAV best) agreed with expert Turing tests better than RadImageNet ones, whose rankings were "volatile and inconsistent". Domain-specific is therefore not automatically better ([Woodland et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13717)). Dermatology encoders exist: PanDerm, pretrained on more than 2M images with linear probing on par with fine-tuning ([Nature Medicine 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-03747-y)), and MONET, a dermatology CLIP with concept-level auditing ([Nature Medicine 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02887-x)). Neither has been validated as a judge of synthetic dermoscopy. The project's DINOv2 probe sinks to df rather than nv, which fits Kang's logic. A linear head, possibly class-weighted, can give the rarest class a large, diffuse region that absorbs off-manifold samples. This is a hypothesis to test by reporting the probe's loss weighting and per-class reliability on real held-out data, not a settled fact. Assembled best practice ([Stein et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04675); [Woodland et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13717); [Ravuri & Vinyals](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10887)) recommends a judge independent of the downstream model, several heterogeneous feature spaces, prior correction, downstream real-test utility over judge agreement, and an expert check on a subset. No formal ensemble-judge protocol for medical synthetic data exists.

## Where ASISM fills documented gaps

| Gap in the literature | Evidence for the gap | What ASISM/E4 contributes | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filters use 1-2 signals with fixed thresholds | HistoGAN, CosSIF, Akrout, ALIA | Five-signal sample-level composite (DINOv2 similarity, IQA, MC-dropout, Grad-CAM, plus classifier signals) | Composite weights cannot be tuned on test results; IQA/MC-dropout/Grad-CAM have no external validation |
| Judge is the downstream classifier or a sibling | Akrout ensemble, confidence filters, pseudo-label bias | DINOv2 judge (J1) independent of the DenseNet | DINOv2 probe has its own df sink, so independence alone is not enough |
| Majority-class sink never documented in dermoscopy | No paper found | Explicit, classifier-specific sink evidence (nv for DenseNet, df for probe) | Needs prior-corrected and k-NN judge variants to localize the cause |
| No per-class quantity sweep on HAM10000 | Jiang (fill-to-head), IOIS (adaptive, no sweep) | E4 pre-registered quantity curve | Rare-class recall rests on a few dozen lesions |
| No FWER control across quantity levels | Azizi, Fan, Jiang, Rehman | Multi-seed runs, Holm across levels | Power is low for df/vasc |
| Leaky splits inflate reported numbers | Galaxy, ham-triage, Cassidy | Lesion-grouped splits, BA as headline | Comparison with published numbers is not like-for-like |

E4's quantity-only design tests one claim directly. If quantity curves peak at different counts for different classes, and those peaks track a per-class generator-quality signal, adaptivity is justified. A uniform curve shape would argue against it. **No paper has done this correlation** ([Fan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04567) and [IOIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08407) come closest). The thesis also inherits obligations the literature makes clear. CosSIF is a required HAM10000 baseline. A memorization or near-duplicate audit of the LoRA generator against training lesions is needed, especially for df and vasc. The judge should be reported with and without logit adjustment. A dermatologist spot check of the disputed synthetic mel images would be the decisive tiebreaker.

## Conclusion

Taken together, the notes suggest that the bottleneck in synthetic HAM10000 augmentation lies in the measurement loop around the generator more than in the generator itself. A judge trained on a 67%-nv prior cannot tell "this synthetic melanoma is bad" apart from "my prior pulls ambiguous inputs to nv". A quantity curve built from single runs cannot tell a peak apart from noise. A filter that maximizes agreement removes the hard samples that carry new information. ASISM's strongest claim is therefore not "five signals beat two". It is that the selection signals, the judges and the count are treated as objects to audit, each with pre-registered tests and a stated failure mode. If E4 returns a null or uniform curve, that is still publishable evidence against the field's default fill-to-head practice on a leakage-free split, so it should not be read as a failed thesis.

The main uncertainties to resolve before writing claims are these. Several quantitative anchors (Azizi's per-row numbers, Ktena's dermatology metrics, the IEEE 2024 macro-recall gain) were not checked against full text. The IQA, MC-dropout and Grad-CAM components lack any precedent in the notes, so the thesis needs its own targeted literature search. The df sink in the DINOv2 probe means the "independent judge" is a family of biased judges whose disagreement, rather than any single verdict, is the most trustworthy signal. Making that disagreement the unit of analysis, and checking it against human experts, is the most defensible original contribution available.

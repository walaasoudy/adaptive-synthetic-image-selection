# Diffusion-model-based synthetic data augmentation for dermatology / skin lesion classification

Research date: 2026-10-02. About 20 tool calls. Many full texts were blocked (Nature, PubMed/PMC captcha, OpenReview verification, large PDFs), so several entries rest on abstracts or search snippets only. These are marked [abstract-only] or [UNVERIFIED]. Where a number is missing, it was not found, not left out.

## Which papers fine-tune Stable Diffusion/SDXL with LoRA or DreamBooth on HAM10000/ISIC, and what gains do they report?

### Takeaway
Several 2023-2026 papers fine-tune Stable Diffusion (SD v1.x; I found no SDXL+LoRA paper on HAM10000 that I could verify) on HAM10000 or ISIC with LoRA, DreamBooth or class-label embeddings. Reported gains vs real-only training are modest, about +1 to +5 points in accuracy or macro recall. Reporting is heterogeneous: accuracy, not balanced accuracy, is the most common headline metric, and per-class or melanoma-recall numbers are often missing.

### Cited Findings
Per-paper records (generator / fine-tuning / dataset / synthetic count / classifier / metrics / gain):

- **SD + LoRA on HAM10000, "Enhancing Skin Cancer Detection Using Synthetic Dermoscopic Images via Stable Diffusion"** (Int. J. Information Technology & Decision Making, World Scientific, DOI 10.1142/S0219622025500567). The authors fine-tune Stable Diffusion with LoRA on HAM10000 to generate synthetic skin cancer images for classifier training. — [World Scientific](https://www.worldscientific.com/doi/10.1142/S0219622025500567) [abstract-only]
- **"Generating Synthetic Images Using Stable Diffusion Model for Skin Lesion Classification"** (IEEE conference, 2024, IEEE doc 10499667). SD is trained on HAM10000 (7 classes). Adding synthetic images at 1-1.5x the real-set size raised macro-average recall by 4-5% and accuracy by about 1-2%, to 90.57% and 89.67% for the two classifiers. — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10499667/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379914871_Generating_Synthetic_Images_Using_Stable_Diffusion_Model_for_Skin_Lesion_Classification). [UNVERIFIED attribution: these numbers came from a search-engine summary that merged several results. They most likely belong to the IEEE paper, but I could not open its full text. Backbone names and per-class counts were not found.]
- **Kim, Yoo, Kwon, Kim et al., "Diffusion-based skin disease data augmentation with fine-grained detail preservation and interpolation for data diversity"**, PLoS ONE 20(10): e0331404, 2025, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0331404.
  - Generator: modified SD v1-4 with an 8-channel VAE, multi-level CLIP adapters, a learnable class-label embedding (1024-d) fed through cross-attention, and latent interpolation between lesion and pseudo-normal pairs. No LoRA or DreamBooth.
  - Data: HAM10000, 7 classes, 500 synthetic images per condition, class-specific sampling (CFG 3-5).
  - Classifiers: VGG13/16/19, ResNet18/34, Swin, ConvNeXt, EVA, CoAtNet.
  - Results: mean accuracy was 80.92% with 500 real images, 76.63% with 500 synthetic only, and 84.15% with 500 real + 500 synthetic (+3.23 pp). FID was 145.26 for baseline SD and 99.21 for the proposed model. IS was 1.77 vs 2.26.
  - Zero-shot accuracy on PAD-UFES-20 was only 49.89-64.27%.
  - Not reported: per-class results and melanoma recall. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12494260/); [PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0331404)
- **Farooq et al., "Derm-T2IM: Harnessing Synthetic Skin Lesion Data via Stable Diffusion Models for Enhanced Skin Disease Classification using ViT and CNN"** (arXiv 2401.05159; IEEE 2024). SD was fine-tuned with DreamBooth and LoRA on 2,800 images (1,400 benign, 1,400 malignant, binary task). The authors report improved robustness of CNN and ViT classifiers across real-world skin cancer datasets. The model is released on Hugging Face and GitHub. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05159); [GitHub](https://github.com/MAli-Farooq/Derm-T2IM) [abstract-only; exact gains not retrieved]
- **Akrout et al., "Diffusion-based Data Augmentation for Skin Disease Classification: Impact Across Original Medical Datasets to Fully Synthetic Images"** (arXiv 2301.04802; MICCAI 2023 workshop proceedings, Springer LNCS, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-53767-7_10).
  - Method: text-to-image diffusion with text-prompt control, applied to a macroscopic (clinical, not dermoscopic) skin disease dataset.
  - Results: synthetic augmentation improved classification. A fully synthetic training set kept accuracy similar to real-only training.
  - Not retrieved: dataset name, counts and backbone. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04802); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-53767-7_10) [abstract-only]
- **Sagers et al. (2022), "Improving Dermatology Classifiers Across Populations Using Images Generated by Large Diffusion Models"** (NeurIPS 2022 Workshop on Synthetic Data). DALL-E 2 (not fine-tuned) generated photorealistic skin-disease images across skin types. Augmenting with them improved classification overall, and especially for underrepresented groups. — [ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/neuripsw/2022/sagers2022neuripsw-improving/); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f63542853f918d905fd109fbc754f90bf2109577). [The dataset is believed to be Fitzpatrick17k: UNVERIFIED, the PDF was blocked.]
- **Sagers, Diao, Melas-Kyriazi, Groh, Rajpurkar, Adamson, Rotemberg, Daneshjou, Manrai (2023), "Augmenting medical image classifiers with synthetic data from latent diffusion models"** (arXiv 2308.12453). The authors generated 458,920 synthetic images with latent diffusion (several generation strategies). Synthetic data helped in data-limited settings, but gains plateaued above a 10:1 synthetic:real ratio. The gains were "substantially smaller than the gains obtained from adding real images". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12453)
- **Ktena, Wiles, Albuquerque, Rebuffi, Tanno, Roy, Azizi, Belgrave, Kohli, Karthikesalingam, Cemgil, Gowal, "Generative models improve fairness of medical classifiers under distribution shifts"**, Nature Medicine 30:1166-1173 (2024), DOI 10.1038/s41591-024-02838-6, arXiv 2304.09218.
  - Method: label-conditioned diffusion models generate samples targeted at underrepresented groups, tested on histopathology, chest X-ray and dermatology.
  - Results: adding synthetic samples improved robustness and fairness both in- and out-of-distribution, with the largest gains for underrepresented groups.
  - Expert check: dermatologists diagnosing the synthetic dermatology images reached accuracy comparable to diagnosing real images. — [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02838-6); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09218); [Europe PMC record PMC11031395](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1038/s41591-024-02838-6&resultType=core&format=json). - **DermDiff (arXiv 2503.17536)**: a diffusion model conditioned on skin tone (from a skin-tone detector) and disease type, using text prompting. It generates dermoscopic images to balance racially imbalanced datasets. Diagnosis is run on real + synthetic data. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.17536); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390142667_DermDiff_Generative_Diffusion_Model_for_Mitigating_Racial_Biases_in_Dermatology_Diagnosis) [abstract-only; numbers not retrieved]
- **Fayyad, Bayasi, Yu, Najjaran, "LesionGen: A Concept-Guided Diffusion Model for Dermatology Image Synthesis"** (MICCAI 2025 ISIC Workshop, arXiv 2507.23001).
  - Method: a pretrained diffusion model fine-tuned on concept-rich captions built from expert annotations plus pseudo-generated reports.
  - Results: training on synthetic data only gives accuracy comparable to real-only training, with gains in worst-case subgroup performance. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.23001) [abstract-only]
- **Wang, Chung, Ding, Hamm, "From Majority to Minority: A Diffusion-based Augmentation for Underrepresented Groups in Skin Lesion Analysis"** (arXiv 2406.18375; Springer LNCS 2024, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-77610-6_2). Diffusion augmentation transfers information from majority skin-type groups to minority groups. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18375); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-77610-6_2) [abstract-only]
- **Filho, Cunha, Papais et al., "DerMAE: Improving skin lesion classification through conditioned latent diffusion and MAE distillation"** (ISBI 2026, arXiv 2602.19848). The pipeline is class-conditioned latent diffusion for imbalanced classes, then MAE pretraining of a large ViT teacher, then distillation to a small ViT. The paper is 4 pages, and no numbers were retrieved. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19848) [abstract-only]
- **Carrión & Norouzi, "Controllable Generation of Diverse Dermatological Imagery for Fair and Efficient Malignancy Classification" (cgDDI)** (MICCAI 2026, arXiv 2607.12987).
  - Data: DDI (656 images) expanded by more than 400x, with 266k+ synthetic images released.
  - Results: malignancy accuracy is 86.4% synthetic-only and 90.9% after fine-tuning on real data. Cross-dataset accuracy on Fitzpatrick17k improves by +13.9%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12987) [abstract-only]
- **Controllable synthesis of dermoscopic images using diffusion models for enhanced computer aided diagnosis and detection**, Medical Image Analysis 2026 (ScienceDirect S1361841526002604). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841526002604) [found in search only; content not retrieved]

### Inferences
- SD with LoRA or DreamBooth on HAM10000 is common, but most of these papers appear in lower-tier venues and headline accuracy, which the nv class dominates. Balanced accuracy and macro-F1 are rarely the primary metric. Cross-paper comparison of "gain" is therefore weak.
- The verified gains on HAM10000 are +3.2 pp mean accuracy (PLoS ONE 2025, in a reduced 500-real-image regime) and +4-5% macro recall (IEEE 2024, unverified attribution). Gains look largest in data-limited regimes, which matches Sagers 2023.

### Gaps
- I found no verifiable SDXL+LoRA paper on HAM10000/ISIC with downstream classifier results.
- I could not retrieve exact dermatology numbers for Ktena 2024 or Sagers 2022/2023, or Derm-T2IM gains.
- I found no Derm7pt-specific diffusion augmentation paper.

## Do synthetic images help minority classes (mel, bkl, df, vasc, akiec, bcc) more than the majority class nv? Are there reported failures or harms?

### Takeaway
The evidence that benefits concentrate in underrepresented groups is mostly about skin tone and demographic subgroups (Ktena, Sagers, LesionGen, From Majority to Minority, cgDDI). Per-diagnosis-class evidence on HAM10000 is thin, and most HAM10000 papers do not report per-class recall. Documented limits include diminishing returns, synthetic data being worth much less than real data, and poor cross-dataset transfer.

### Cited Findings
- Benefits were largest for underrepresented groups and out-of-distribution, in all three modalities including dermatology. — [Ktena et al. 2024, Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02838-6)
- DALL-E 2 augmentation improved classification "especially for underrepresented groups" (skin types). — [Sagers et al. 2022](https://mlanthology.org/neuripsw/2022/sagers2022neuripsw-improving/)
- Gains plateau above a 10:1 synthetic:real ratio and are much smaller than adding real images. — [Sagers et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12453)
- SkinGenBench (Pritam, Shiney O, Jain; arXiv 2512.17585, Dec 2025, rev. Apr 2026):
  - Setup: StyleGAN2-ADA vs DDPM on 14,116 images from HAM10000 + MILK10K, 5 classes, 5 classifiers.
  - Results: melanoma F1 rose by 8-15 points absolute. The best model, ViT-B/16, reached F1 about 0.88 and ROC-AUC about 0.98, roughly 14% over non-augmented training. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17585)
- Training on 500 synthetic images only gave lower accuracy than 500 real (76.63% vs 80.92%). Cross-dataset transfer to PAD-UFES-20 was weak (49.89-64.27%). — [Kim et al. 2025, PLoS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12494260/)
- Gains in worst-case subgroup performance with synthetic-only training. — [LesionGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.23001)

### Inferences
- No retrieved paper directly shows per-class HAM10000 gains for df, vasc or akiec vs nv. The minority-benefit claim for diagnosis classes is plausible but not well documented in what I could access.
- Possible harm: if a generator does not separate classes well (e.g., synthetic mel looks like nv), adding it can shift decision boundaries toward the majority class. This is consistent with this project's own V2/V3 diagnostics. I found no published paper that names this specific failure for HAM10000.

### Gaps
- I found no paper reporting a net degradation in balanced accuracy on HAM10000 from diffusion augmentation. Absence of evidence may reflect publication bias.
- Per-class melanoma recall vs real-only is missing for most SD/LoRA HAM10000 papers.

## How are synthetic dermoscopy images evaluated (FID, KID, expert reader studies, downstream accuracy)?

### Takeaway
Papers usually combine FID, sometimes KID, IS or LPIPS/MS-SSIM, with downstream classifier accuracy. Expert reader studies are rare; Ktena 2024 is the main high-profile one. Absolute FID values vary widely, from about 65 to 145, and are not comparable across papers.

### Cited Findings
- FID was 145.26 for baseline SD and 99.21 for the proposed model, with IS 1.77 vs 2.26 and LPIPS/MS-SSIM for VAE reconstruction. CLIP-embedding semantic variance was used as a diversity measure. — [Kim et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12494260/)
- StyleGAN2-ADA reached FID about 65.5 and KID about 0.05. StyleGAN2-ADA images aligned more closely with the real distribution, while DDPM produced higher-variance samples with lower perceptual fidelity. Generator choice mattered more than preprocessing. — [SkinGenBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17585)
- Dermatologists' diagnostic accuracy on synthetic images was comparable to their accuracy on real images. — [Ktena et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02838-6)
- Carrasco Limeros et al. assessed fidelity, diversity, training efficiency, classifier performance and latent-space analysis. The distance between a real image and its latent projection served as an authenticity / memorization indicator (StyleGAN2-ADA, ISIC 2020, binary benign/malignant, centralized and 3-hospital federated settings). — [arXiv 2208.11702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11702)

### Inferences
- FID uses Inception features trained on ImageNet and may not track diagnostic fidelity. Downstream utility and class-separability checks, such as classifier or DINOv2 probes on synthetic images, are more informative and still underused in the literature.

### Gaps
- I found no standardized benchmark of FID or KID across HAM10000 generators. Reader-study protocols (visual Turing tests) for HAM10000 SD/LoRA outputs were not found.

## What known failure modes exist (memorization, artifacts like rulers/hair, lack of diversity, label leakage)?

### Takeaway
The documented failure modes are:
- memorization or near-duplication, which is stronger for diffusion models than for GANs
- reproduced acquisition artifacts: rulers, hair, ink markings
- over-emphasis of hair or colour cues
- trade-offs between fidelity and diversity
- poor out-of-domain transfer

Heavy artifact-removal preprocessing gives little benefit and may remove useful texture.

### Cited Findings
- StyleGAN2-ADA on ISIC 2020 generated artifacts, reproduced rulers, and suffered hair interference. — [Carrasco Limeros et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11702)
- Excessive emphasis on hair regions or specific lesion colours caused failures. The authors applied DullRazor hair removal. Generating pseudo-normal skin failed when the lesion and normal regions differed a lot. — [Kim et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12494260/)
- Advanced artifact removal gave only marginal improvements in generative metrics, limited downstream gains, and may suppress clinically relevant texture. — [SkinGenBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17585)
- Unconditional latent diffusion models memorize patient imaging data. About 37.2% of patient data was detected as memorized and 68.7% of synthetic samples were identified as copies of patient data across the datasets studied. Augmentation, smaller models and larger datasets reduce memorization; over-training increases it. — [Nature Biomedical Engineering 2025, "Unconditional latent diffusion models memorize patient imaging data"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-025-01468-8); [arXiv 2402.01054](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01054). [These datasets are not dermatology-specific, as far as the snippet shows.]
- Diffusion models memorize training images much more than GANs (brain MRI, chest X-ray). — [Beware of diffusion models..., MLST / arXiv 2305.07644](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.07644)
- MemControl proposes automated parameter selection for fine-tuning medical diffusion models to limit memorization. — [arXiv 2405.19458](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19458v1)
- Diffusion samples show higher variance but lower fidelity than StyleGAN2-ADA; GAN samples stay closer to the real distribution. — [SkinGenBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17585)

### Inferences
- LoRA or DreamBooth fine-tuning on small minority classes (HAM10000 df has 115 images and vasc 142) is exactly the regime where memorization risk is highest. Near-duplicate checks against training images are needed.
- Label leakage, where synthetic images generated from test-set or validation-set images contaminate evaluation, was not explicitly documented in the retrieved papers. It is a standard concern when generators are trained on the full dataset before the train/test split. HAM10000 also has multiple images per lesion (lesion_id), so the split should be made by lesion.

### Gaps
- I found no dermatology-specific memorization audit of SD/LoRA HAM10000 models.
- I found no paper explicitly measuring label leakage or lesion-ID leakage through the generator in HAM10000.
